# Reinforcement Learning

## Temporal differences and action values

- **Temporal-difference learning** - Learns predictions from differences between successive predictions, rather than waiting only for final outcomes. Establishes convergence and optimality results for specified cases. [Sutton 1988](<https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00115009> "Learning to predict by the methods of temporal differences; Mach. Learn.").
- **Q-learning** - Incrementally estimates action values from experience. Proves convergence under the paper's tabular representation, sampling, and convergence assumptions; this is not a deep-network convergence guarantee. [Watkins and Dayan 1992](<https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00992698> "Q-learning; Mach. Learn.").
- **DQN** - Combines convolutional visual representations with Q-learning to estimate future reward from game pixels. The original preprint studies seven Atari games; later DQN evaluations use different protocols and game sets. [Mnih et al. 2013](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.5602> "Playing Atari with Deep Reinforcement Learning; NeurIPS DL Workshop").
- **Double DQN** - Separates action selection from action evaluation in the Q-learning target. Reduces overestimation arising from using the same estimates to select and evaluate an action. [van Hasselt et al. 2016](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06461> "Deep Reinforcement Learning with Double Q-learning; AAAI").
- **Dueling networks** - Splits the action-value network into state-value and action-advantage estimators. Shares learning across actions without requiring a different underlying reinforcement-learning algorithm. [Wang et al. 2015](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06581> "Dueling Network Architectures for Deep Reinforcement Learning; arXiv").
- **Prioritized experience replay** - Samples selected stored transitions more often instead of replaying all experience uniformly. Changes which past interactions drive learning, rather than how the agent collects new data. [Schaul et al. 2016](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05952> "Prioritized Experience Replay; ICLR").
- **Rainbow** - Combines six DQN extensions and measures their contributions through ablations. Demonstrates that improvements need to be tested jointly; isolated gains need not simply add together. [Hessel et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02298> "Rainbow: Combining Improvements in Deep Reinforcement Learning; arXiv").

## Return distributions

- **Distributional reinforcement learning** - Models the distribution of random returns instead of only their expectation. Develops distributional Bellman analysis and a practical approximate-distribution algorithm, while identifying instability in the control setting. [Bellemare et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06887> "A Distributional Perspective on Reinforcement Learning; ICML").
- **Quantile regression DQN** - Represents the return distribution through quantiles and learns them with quantile regression. Connects approximate distribution learning to a distributional Bellman formulation. [Dabney et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10044> "Distributional Reinforcement Learning with Quantile Regression; arXiv").

## Policy gradients and actor–critic methods

- **REINFORCE** - Derives stochastic-network updates aligned with the gradient of expected reinforcement. Covers immediate rewards and specified delayed-reward settings, and connects the updates with backpropagation. [Williams 1992](<https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00992696> "Simple statistical gradient-following algorithms for connectionist reinforcement learning; Mach. Learn.").
- **TRPO** - Optimizes a policy surrogate with a constraint on policy divergence. The practical algorithm approximates a theoretically justified update; monotonic improvement is not an unconditional implementation guarantee. [Schulman et al. 2015](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05477> "Trust Region Policy Optimization; ICML").
- **Generalized advantage estimation** - Uses an exponentially weighted advantage estimator to reduce policy-gradient variance at the cost of bias. Connects advantage estimation to temporal-difference learning with eligibility traces. [Schulman et al. 2015](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02438> "High-Dimensional Continuous Control Using Generalized Advantage Estimation; arXiv").
- **A3C** - Runs parallel actor-learners with asynchronous parameter updates. Studies how diverse concurrent experience stabilizes deep value and actor-critic learning. [Mnih et al. 2016](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.01783> "Asynchronous Methods for Deep Reinforcement Learning; ICML").
- **PPO** - Alternates interaction with minibatch optimization of a policy surrogate. Its clipped-ratio variant limits incentives for large updates; clipping is not a hard bound on policy divergence. [Schulman et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347> "Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms; arXiv").
- **DDPG** - Uses a deterministic policy gradient within an actor-critic architecture for continuous actions. Extends deep Q-learning ideas to settings where enumerating all actions is impractical. [Lillicrap et al. 2015](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02971> "Continuous control with deep reinforcement learning; arXiv").
- **TD3** - Uses the minimum of two critic estimates and delays policy updates to reduce effects of approximation error. Addresses overestimated values in continuous-action actor-critic learning. [Fujimoto et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.09477> "Addressing Function Approximation Error in Actor-Critic Methods; ICML").
- **Soft Actor-Critic** - Optimizes reward together with policy entropy using an off-policy stochastic actor-critic. The entropy term encourages varied actions while the critic estimates task value. [Haarnoja et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01290> "Soft Actor-Critic: Off-Policy Maximum Entropy Deep Reinforcement Learning with a Stochastic Actor; ICML").
- **SAC temperature adaptation** - Extends Soft Actor-Critic with a constrained formulation that tunes the entropy temperature automatically. Studies sensitivity to settings and applications in simulated and physical control. [Haarnoja et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05905> "Soft Actor-Critic Algorithms and Applications; arXiv").
- **Control as inference** - Connects maximum-entropy control with probabilistic inference. Distinguishes exact inference under deterministic dynamics from a variational treatment under stochastic dynamics. [Levine 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00909> "Reinforcement Learning and Control as Probabilistic Inference: Tutorial and Review; arXiv").

## Exploration and goal relabeling

- **Density-based pseudo-counts** - Derives visitation-like counts from density models to extend count-based exploration beyond tabular states. Converts estimated novelty into intrinsic rewards. [Bellemare et al. 2016](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.01868> "Unifying Count-Based Exploration and Intrinsic Motivation; NeurIPS").
- **Intrinsic curiosity module** - Uses prediction error in an inverse-dynamics-trained feature space as an intrinsic reward. Encourages exploration through failures to predict action consequences rather than raw pixel reconstruction alone. [Pathak et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.05363> "Curiosity-driven Exploration by Self-supervised Prediction; ICML").
- **Random network distillation** - Rewards prediction error against features from a fixed random network. Introduces a novelty signal and a mechanism for combining intrinsic and task rewards. [Burda et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12894> "Exploration by Random Network Distillation; arXiv").
- **NoisyNet** - Adds parameterized noise to network weights and learns its parameters with the task objective. Produces exploratory policy variation through the network rather than only random action replacement. [Fortunato et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.10295> "Noisy Networks for Exploration; ICLR").
- **Hindsight experience replay** - Reuses unsuccessful goal-conditioned episodes by relabeling them with goals actually achieved. Recomputes the associated rewards to learn from sparse binary feedback. [Andrychowicz et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01495> "Hindsight Experience Replay; NeurIPS").

## Goal-conditioned representations and planning

- **Contrastive RL** - Contrasts future states with random states for each state-action pair. The optimal critic encodes discounted future-state occupancy ratios; maximizing its score trains a goal-reaching policy. [Eysenbach et al. 2022](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07568> "Contrastive Learning as Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning; NeurIPS").
- **Search on the Replay Buffer** - Builds a graph of visited states with goal-value-based distance estimates, then searches for subgoals a local policy can reach. Planning depends on replay coverage and distance accuracy; evaluated on image navigation. [Eysenbach et al. 2019](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05253> "Search on the Replay Buffer: Bridging Planning and Reinforcement Learning; NeurIPS").
- **HIQL** - Learns an offline goal-value function, a high-level policy predicting latent subgoals and a low-level controller. Extends IQL to hierarchical goal reaching; value learning can use action-free trajectories, while controller extraction needs actions. [Park et al. 2023](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11949> "HIQL: Offline Goal-Conditioned RL with Latent States as Actions; NeurIPS").
- **Inference via Interpolation** - Its temporal contrastive parameterization yields a Gauss-Markov chain under Gaussian-marginal and optimal-critic assumptions. Infers latent waypoints from offline trajectories; no online control-policy learning. [Eysenbach et al. 2024](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04082> "Inference via Interpolation: Contrastive Representations Provably Enable Planning and Inference; NeurIPS").
- **C-Slerp** - Extends contrastive planning to online RL: rotation-aware spherical interpolation generates latent waypoints and adapts their count. Uses no separate high-level network or graph search. [Park et al. 2026](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20409> "Hyperspherical Contrastive Representations Unlock Planning and Exploration; arXiv"); [code](<https://github.com/jonghaepark/c-slerp>).
- **C-Slerp assumptions** - The waypoint derivation assumes a Bayes-optimal critic and uniform spherical marginals, then uses a small-variance approximation. Closed-form latent waypoints do not establish feasible paths for imperfect learned representations. [Park et al. 2026](<https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20409v1#S4.SS3> "Hyperspherical Contrastive Representations Unlock Planning and Exploration; arXiv").
- **C-Slerp evidence** - Reports higher success with adaptive than fixed counts on 8-tower and Ant U5 Maze. Five-seed, state-based simulations; eight cubes use direct forces, gripper tasks use up to five. Visual inputs and physical robots remain untested. [Park et al. 2026](<https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20409v1#S5.SS1> "Hyperspherical Contrastive Representations Unlock Planning and Exploration; arXiv").

## Memory and temporal abstraction

- **Deep recurrent Q-networks** - Adds an LSTM to DQN to integrate observations over time. Studies partial observability and changing observation quality; recurrence does not show a systematic advantage on every fully observed task. [Hausknecht and Stone 2015](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.06527> "Deep Recurrent Q-Learning for Partially Observable MDPs; arXiv").
- **Option-Critic** - Learns temporally extended actions through their internal policies and termination rules, together with a policy over options. Does not require externally specified subgoals or extra reward signals. [Bacon et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05140> "The Option-Critic Architecture; AAAI").
- **HIRO** - Learns high-level goals and lower-level goal-conditioned control using off-policy data. Corrects higher-level replay for changes in the lower-level behavior that alter what a goal accomplishes. [Nachum et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08296> "Data-Efficient Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning; NeurIPS").
- **DIAYN** - Learns diverse skills without task rewards using an information-theoretic objective and maximum-entropy policies. Studies skill pretraining and hierarchical composition for later tasks. [Eysenbach et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06070> "Diversity is All You Need: Learning Skills without a Reward Function; arXiv").
- **Successor features** - Separates expected future feature occupancy from reward weights, enabling policy transfer across tasks with shared dynamics and different rewards; paired with generalized policy improvement. [Barreto et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05312> "Successor Features for Transfer in Reinforcement Learning; NeurIPS").

## Learned dynamics and planning

- **Linear Dyna and prioritized sweeping** - Learns from imagined transitions generated by a world model. Extends Dyna planning and prioritized sweeping to linear function approximation, with convergence results under stated conditions. [Sutton et al. 2008](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.3285> "Dyna-Style Planning with Linear Function Approximation and Prioritized Sweeping; UAI").
- **PETS** - Combines probabilistic ensembles of dynamics models with trajectory sampling to propagate uncertainty during planning. Studies model uncertainty as a practical component of data-efficient control. [Chua et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12114> "Deep Reinforcement Learning in a Handful of Trials using Probabilistic Dynamics Models; NeurIPS").
- **Model-Based Policy Optimization** - Uses short model-generated rollouts branching from real observations. Limits reliance on long simulated trajectories, where accumulated model error can undermine policy optimization. [Janner et al. 2019](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08253> "When to Trust Your Model: Model-Based Policy Optimization; NeurIPS").
- **AlphaZero** - Combines neural policy/value prediction, Monte Carlo tree search, and self-play for board games. Uses the game rules for planning; learning without human games does not mean learning without a simulator. [Silver et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01815> "Mastering Chess and Shogi by Self-Play with a General Reinforcement Learning Algorithm; arXiv").
- **MuZero** - Learns a model predicting reward, policy, and value for tree search. Optimizes planning-relevant quantities rather than requiring a model that reconstructs every detail of future observations. [Schrittwieser et al. 2020](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265> "Mastering Atari, Go, Chess and Shogi by Planning with a Learned Model; Nature").
- **Dreamer** - Learns behavior by propagating value gradients through imagined trajectories in a compact latent world model. Separates learning environmental dynamics from collecting every policy update through real interaction. [Hafner et al. 2019](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01603> "Dream to Control: Learning Behaviors by Latent Imagination; arXiv").
- **DreamerV2** - Uses discrete latent world-model representations and learns behavior from model predictions. Trains the world model separately from the policy and studies both Atari and continuous control. [Hafner et al. 2021](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02193> "Mastering Atari with Discrete World Models; ICLR").
- **DreamerV3** - Combines latent imagination with normalization, balancing, and transformation choices intended to stabilize learning across domains. Evaluates a shared configuration across diverse tasks; no universal task guarantee follows. [Hafner et al. 2023](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104> "Mastering Diverse Domains through World Models; arXiv").
- **TD-MPC2** - Combines a decoder-free latent world model with local trajectory optimization for continuous control, including multitask training across tasks with different action spaces. [Hansen et al. 2024](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828> "TD-MPC2: Scalable, Robust World Models for Continuous Control; ICLR").

## Learning from fixed datasets

- **Batch-Constrained Q-learning** - Restricts candidate actions to remain close to those supported by a fixed dataset. Addresses extrapolation errors that arise when offline policy learning evaluates poorly supported actions. [Fujimoto et al. 2019](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02900> "Off-Policy Deep Reinforcement Learning without Exploration; ICML").
- **Behavior-regularized actor-critic** - Organizes offline RL around regularizing policy behavior relative to logged data. Uses shared comparisons and ablations to examine which algorithmic choices matter. [Wu et al. 2019](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.11361> "Behavior Regularized Offline Reinforcement Learning; arXiv").
- **Conservative Q-learning** - Adds a value regularizer to Bellman-error training to learn conservative estimates from offline data. Lower-bound results have assumptions; conservative training does not guarantee deployment performance. [Kumar et al. 2020](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04779> "Conservative Q-Learning for Offline Reinforcement Learning; NeurIPS").
- **Implicit Q-learning** - Fits an expectile state-value estimate, backs it into a Q-function, and extracts a policy through advantage-weighted cloning. Avoids querying unseen actions during value learning. [Kostrikov et al. 2021](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06169> "Offline Reinforcement Learning with Implicit Q-Learning; arXiv").
- **Decision Transformer** - Predicts actions autoregressively from desired return, past states, and actions. Recasts offline policy learning as conditional sequence modeling; requesting a return does not guarantee achieving it. [Chen et al. 2021](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.01345> "Decision Transformer: Reinforcement Learning via Sequence Modeling; NeurIPS").
- **Diffuser** - Plans by iteratively denoising trajectories, combining learned generation with conditioning on goals or rewards. Moves part of trajectory optimization into the learned model. [Janner et al. 2022](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09991> "Planning with Diffusion for Flexible Behavior Synthesis; ICML").
- **Cal-QL** - Calibrates conservative offline value estimates against a reference policy to improve initialization for subsequent online learning; addresses the offline-to-online transition. [Nakamoto et al. 2023](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05479> "Cal-QL: Calibrated Offline RL Pre-Training for Efficient Online Fine-Tuning; NeurIPS").

## Off-policy evaluation

- **Sequential doubly robust evaluation** - Combines importance weighting with value-model corrections to estimate a target policy's return from logged behavior. Jiang and Li reduce variance in studied tasks; requires policy probabilities and action support. [Jiang and Li 2016](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03722> "Doubly Robust Off-policy Value Evaluation for Reinforcement Learning; ICML").
- **Fitted Q Evaluation** - Repeatedly regresses Bellman targets for a fixed policy on logged transitions, then estimates its value. Le et al.'s error bounds require coverage and function-class conditions; fitting low regression error alone does not certify a new policy. [Le et al. 2019](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08738> "Batch Policy Learning under Constraints; arXiv").

## Demonstrations and preference feedback

- **DAgger** - Aggregates expert labels on observations encountered by the learner's policy. Addresses distribution shift between expert demonstrations and the states visited after the learner makes mistakes. [Ross et al. 2011](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686> "A Reduction of Imitation Learning and Structured Prediction to No-Regret Online Learning; AISTATS").
- **GAIL** - Learns a policy through adversarial comparison with expert behavior, without a supplied task reward. The original method still collects policy rollouts; it is not purely offline behavioral cloning. [Ho and Ermon 2016](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03476> "Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning; NeurIPS").
- **Human preference rewards** - Learns task objectives from human comparisons of trajectory segments and trains policies using that feedback. Replaces hand-written task rewards with learned preferences, not with direct access to human intent. [Christiano et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03741> "Deep reinforcement learning from human preferences; NeurIPS").
- **Learning with demonstrations and long horizons** - Combines a transformed Bellman operator, temporal consistency, and human demonstrations for Atari. Separates reward-scale handling, long-horizon learning, and exploration contributions. [Pohlen et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11593> "Observe and Look Further: Achieving Consistent Performance on Atari; arXiv").

## Multiple agents and constrained control

- **MADDPG** - Extends actor-critic learning to account for other agents' actions during training. Studies cooperative and competitive interaction, where other learning agents make the environment nonstationary. [Lowe et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02275> "Multi-Agent Actor-Critic for Mixed Cooperative-Competitive Environments; NeurIPS").
- **QMIX** - Combines per-agent values with a monotonic mixing network for centralized training and decentralized action choice. The monotonicity restriction makes local greedy choices consistent with the joint value model. [Rashid et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.11485> "QMIX: Monotonic Value Function Factorisation for Deep Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning; arXiv").
- **Multi-agent RL review** - Organizes challenges including nonstationarity, partial observability, communication, and centralized versus decentralized training. Surveys approaches and their application settings. [Nguyen et al. 2020](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.11794> "Deep Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Agent Systems: A Review of Challenges, Solutions and Applications; IEEE Trans. Cybern.").
- **Constrained Policy Optimization** - Optimizes reward subject to expected-cost constraints through a policy-search update. Theoretical near-constraint satisfaction depends on assumptions and does not certify physical safety of a learned controller. [Achiam et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10528> "Constrained Policy Optimization; ICML").
- **Evolution strategies for control** - Optimizes policy parameters through black-box evaluations and perturbations. Provides a control-learning baseline that does not require value functions or policy-gradient credit assignment through actions. [Salimans et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864> "Evolution Strategies as a Scalable Alternative to Reinforcement Learning; arXiv").

## Evaluation and datasets

- **Reproducibility in deep RL** - Shows how nondeterminism, hyperparameters, and reporting choices complicate algorithm comparisons. Calls for standardized protocols and uncertainty reporting rather than isolated best-run scores. [Henderson et al. 2018](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06560> "Deep Reinforcement Learning that Matters; AAAI").
- **Arcade Learning Environment protocols** - Reviews Atari evaluation practices and introduces sticky actions as environmental stochasticity. Makes protocol differences explicit when comparing game-playing agents. [Machado et al. 2017](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06009> "Revisiting the Arcade Learning Environment: Evaluation Protocols and Open Problems for General Agents; arXiv").
- **Statistical uncertainty in RL benchmarks** - Shows that few-run point estimates can change conclusions about algorithm rankings. Recommends interval estimates, performance profiles, and robust aggregates such as the interquartile mean. [Agarwal et al. 2021](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264> "Deep Reinforcement Learning at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice; NeurIPS").
- **D4RL** - Benchmarks offline learning with human, controller-generated, multitask, and mixed-policy datasets. Tests limitations hidden by datasets collected only from partially trained RL agents. [Fu et al. 2020](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07219> "D4RL: Datasets for Deep Data-Driven Reinforcement Learning; arXiv").
- **Off-policy evaluation benchmark** - Stress-tests methods that estimate a policy's performance from data collected by other policies. Studies how experimental conditions affect estimator reliability and provides the COBS benchmark. [Voloshin et al. 2019](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06854> "Empirical Study of Off-Policy Policy Evaluation for Reinforcement Learning; arXiv").

## Software

- **Stable-Baselines3** - Provides Python implementations with consistent interfaces, documentation, and comparisons to reference codebases. A practical baseline implementation collection; the cited paper describes its tested release. [Raffin et al. 2021](<https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v22/20-1364.html> "Stable-Baselines3: Reliable Reinforcement Learning Implementations; JMLR").
- **CleanRL** - Provides self-contained algorithm implementations in single files, including training details. Supports inspection and modification of an individual algorithm without tracing a large shared framework. [Huang et al. 2022](<https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v23/21-1342.html> "CleanRL: High-quality Single-file Implementations of Deep Reinforcement Learning Algorithms; JMLR").
